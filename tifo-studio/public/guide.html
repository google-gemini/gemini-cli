How Tifo-style videos are made

Tifo Football (now part of The Athletic) makes 6–15 minute explainers that mix a calm, authoritative voiceover with flat, textured illustrations and animated tactics boards. Here is the production pipeline behind that look, and how each step maps onto this studio.

1. What makes the look

  • Flat, limited palette — muted grass greens, cream pitch lines, 2–3 strong accent colours (team colours + a highlight yellow). No gradients or 3D.
  • Texture — a fine paper/film grain over everything and a soft vignette so it feels printed, not digital.
  • Tactics board — a top-down pitch with players as numbered discs, dashed pass lines, solid run arrows that draw themselves on, shaded zones for space, lines linking a back four.
  • Camera moves — slow zooms and pans across the board to follow the ball, instead of cuts.
  • Illustration cards — hand-drawn caricatures of players/managers and scenes, with slow push-ins and parallax.
  • Bold condensed type — uppercase condensed fonts (Oswald / Bebas-like) for titles and labels.
  • Voice first — every visual beat is timed to the narration. The script drives everything.

2. The production pipeline

01Research & script. A writer researches the topic (match footage, data from FBref/Opta/StatsBomb, interviews) and writes a 1,000–2,000 word script. Each paragraph is roughly one visual idea.
In the studio: Script → Scenes splits your script into one scene per paragraph, and can voice every scene straight away.
02Voiceover. The script is recorded first, cleanly, in a quiet room. The recording locks the timing of the whole video.
In the studio: 🤖 Generate voice uses a free local neural voice (British Piper narrators) and fits each scene to it. Or ● Record mic (the animation plays while you talk), or Upload audio, for example from Audacity or ElevenLabs.
03Storyboard / animatic. Rough frames for each line of the script: which players, which arrows, where the camera goes.
In the studio: rough out each scene with Insert XI and a few arrows, then refine.
04Illustration. Illustrators draw player caricatures, crowds, stadiums and title cards in Adobe Illustrator/Photoshop (or Procreate), with layered textures.
In the studio: a Card scene (title, chapter, quote or lower-third layout) with your illustration. The Illustrated filter turns photos into flat, outlined poster art, and a transparent foreground PNG gets parallax depth. Portraits can go on player discs. Free drawing tools: Krita, Inkscape, Photopea.
05Animation. Everything is assembled in Adobe After Effects: player discs keyframed across the board, arrows revealed with Trim Paths, zones scaling in, camera moves, texture overlays.
In the studio: move the playhead and drag players to create eased keyframes. Arrows draw themselves on. Cover shadows, spotlights, team shapes, trails and distance lines are one click each. The camera zooms, tilts into 3D and rotates between keyframes, like Tifo's angled pitch shots. Charts animate stats, and wipe or crossfade transitions join the scenes.
06Sound design & music. A subtle music bed under the voice, plus whooshes and crowd sounds.
In the studio: automatic sound effects (whooshes on transitions and camera moves, ticks when graphics appear) and background music with a fade-out, both in Project & Teams. Free music: YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay Music.
07Export & publish. 1080p H.264 upload, with subtitles and a bold thumbnail.
In the studio: Export Video gives a frame-accurate MP4 at 1080p, 1440p or 4K, at 30 or 60 fps. 📷 Snapshot makes the thumbnail and Subtitles .srt the captions.

4. Tips for the Tifo feel

  • One idea per scene. Remove everything that isn't being talked about (use Disappears).
  • Hold, then move. Let the viewer read the setup for a second before anything moves — press ◆ Key at the moment movement should start.
  • Use “Continue ▸” so the next scene starts exactly where the last ended; it looks like one continuous board.
  • Zoom sparingly — 1.5×–2.5× on the action, then back out to show the shape.
  • Highlight the protagonist with the pulse ring, and give key players name tags.
  • Stick to 2 team colours + 1 highlight colour.
  • Intercut illustration cards between tactical scenes for pacing (manager portrait, stadium, a quote).

5. Converting & finishing

If your browser exported WebM, convert to MP4 for editors/YouTube with ffmpeg:

ffmpeg -i video.webm -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a aac -b:a 192k video.mp4

Want more polish? Export individual scenes and finish in a free editor like DaVinci Resolve, Kdenlive or CapCut: add sound effects, crowd noise, and footage clips (only use footage you have the rights to).
